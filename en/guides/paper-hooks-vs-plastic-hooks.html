Paper Hooks vs Plastic Hooks for Retail Packaging

By KALOZEN · How to compare hook materials for sock and apparel packaging

When specifying a hang tab for sock or apparel packaging, buyers often compare die-cut paper hooks with plastic hooks. The right choice depends on the retail format, brand presentation and the specifications you can confirm with a sample.

1. Material and product form

A paper hook is die-cut from paperboard and can carry the same print as the packaging it attaches to. A plastic hook is typically moulded as a separate component. This guide compares the two on the factors buyers actually specify; it does not claim one is universally better.

FactorDie-cut paper hookPlastic hook
MaterialPaperboard, grade confirmed by structure and sampleMoulded plastic resin
PrintingPrints with the pack/header as one visual surfaceUsually a separate, unprinted component
ShapeDie-cut outlines such as S, J/question-mark, 2-shapeMoulded standard forms
SpecificationsConfirm board, thickness, hole, bar fit by drawing and sampleConfirm moulded dimensions and fit

2. What to check in a display context

  • Rail compatibility: confirm the bar diameter the hook must sit on, whatever the material.
  • Load requirement: state the packed weight; do not assume a rating without a confirmed sample.
  • Visual consistency: if the hook should match the pack artwork, a printed paper hook keeps one surface.
  • End-of-life handling: confirm local requirements with your own compliance team; KALOZEN does not make unverified environmental claims.
KALOZEN coordinates eco paper hooks (H01–H08) as customisable directions. Board grade, thickness, hole structure and load are Custom Specifications confirmed by drawing and sample — not fixed catalogue values.
