How to Choose Paper Hooks for Sock Packaging

By KALOZEN · Practical guidance for buyers specifying eco paper hooks

Eco paper hooks replace plastic hangers on sock packaging. They are die-cut from paperboard, printed with brand and size information, and let a packed pair of socks hang from a retail display rail. Choosing the right hook starts with how the product will be displayed, not with a fixed catalogue size.

1. Start with the display method

Confirm how the hook will be used before choosing a shape:

  • Display rail or peg: a question-mark (J) hook gives an open throat for fast hanging and restocking.
  • Header-card attachment: a standard S or one-piece hook can be fixed to a printed header card.
  • Heavier multi-pair packs: a reinforced shape with a wider shoulder spreads load across the board.

KALOZEN's H01–H08 paper hook range covers standard die-cut, heavy-duty, question-mark and custom die-cut directions. Each style is a customisable option, not a confirmed stock SKU.

2. Specifications to confirm before sampling

Paper hook performance depends on a few variables. Provide or confirm the following in your inquiry:

  • Board material and thickness — selected by structure and intended load.
  • Hole structure and bar fit — the diameter of the retail rail the hook must sit on.
  • Throat width and hook outline — set by your artwork and pack layout.
  • Printing and finishing — brand colours, size markings and any surface treatment.
Exact values are not assumed. Unconfirmed dimensions, materials and load figures are marked Custom Specifications or To be confirmed, and are settled by drawing and approved sample.

3. How samples are confirmed

A typical sequence is: share the intended use and hook direction → confirm structure and artwork layout → review a drawing or digital sample → approve a physical sample → confirm production details with the manufacturing partner. KALOZEN coordinates specification confirmation, sample approval and delivery through partner factories; Zhengzhou is the business coordination location, not a self-owned factory.

4. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Specifying a hook shape without confirming the rail diameter it must fit.
  • Assuming a load rating without a confirmed board grade and sample test.
  • Treating a style name as a ready-made size — all styles are customisable directions.
